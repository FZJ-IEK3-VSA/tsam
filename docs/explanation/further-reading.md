# Further Reading

ETHOS.TSAM was originally built to cut the computational load of large-scale energy system
optimization models, and most of the literature below comes from that setting — though the
methods themselves apply to any time series.

If you are new to the field, start with **Hoffmann et al. (2020)**, a review of time series
aggregation methods that also supplies the notation this documentation follows. From there,
**Kotzur et al. (2018a)** validates the methods against cost-optimal results across several
energy system use cases; **Kotzur et al. (2018b)** introduces a way to model state variables
(such as a storage component's state of charge) *between* aggregated typical periods;
**Kannengießer et al. (2019)** investigates how far aggregation can simplify mixed-integer
linear problems; and **Kotzur et al. (2021)** sets aggregation alongside the other ways of
handling complexity in energy system models.

## Citing ETHOS.TSAM

If you use ETHOS.TSAM in scientific work, please cite **Hoffmann et al. (2022)**.
